2027 FEHB Open Season: Choosing the Right Health Plan in Japan

Nov 17, 2026 · Written by Dr. Kenji Taylor, MD · 4 min read

Living in Japan changes how you use your American health insurance. Your plan should make healthcare easier, not more complicated.

Part 1: Three things to consider before you enroll

Open Season: November 9 – December 14, 2026. This is the one time each year to review and change your Federal Employees Health Benefits (FEHB) plan for 2027.

Two popular options for federal employees living in Japan are Blue Cross Blue Shield Federal (FEP Blue) and the Foreign Service Benefit Plan (FSBP), administered by Aetna (not to be confused with Aetna-Allianz, the NAF employee plan).

Both offer overseas coverage, but important differences in out-of-pocket costs, claims processing, and access to care aren't always obvious when comparing premiums.

At Torii Health–Iris Dental Medical, we've worked extensively with both plans over the past year. Here are three things we've learned that may help you choose the right coverage.

1. Routine Care, Medications, Imaging, and Procedures: What's Covered?

FEP Blue Basic and Focus waive office-visit copays overseas, but costs for labs, imaging, procedures, medications, and hospitalizations can vary considerably by plan and service.

Prescription coverage can be particularly complicated, with multiple drug tiers, coverage restrictions, and pharmacy benefits that may be difficult to access while living in Japan.

FSBP generally covers 90% of eligible outpatient services, including visits, labs, imaging, and procedures after a modest deductible. It also offers generous benefits for medications, preventive care, hospitalization, massage therapy, and nutritional counseling, subject to plan-specific limits.

In our experience, FSBP's coverage and reimbursement are more predictable, while FEP Blue claims can involve more complex cost-sharing and reimbursement rules.

2. Direct Billing: Who Handles the Paperwork?

Most healthcare providers in Japan require patients to pay upfront and submit their own insurance claims.

Billing US insurers directly is complicated, costly, and requires specialized staff to manage claims, follow up on payments, and resolve denials. It also creates additional financial risk for healthcare providers.

At Torii Health–Iris Dental Medical, we handle that paperwork for you, helping reduce upfront costs and administrative burdens. We offer direct billing for eligible medical and counseling services with both FSBP and FEP Blue.

In our experience, FSBP's overseas billing process has been particularly straightforward, with fewer denials and requests for additional documentation.

3. Your Family's Healthcare Needs: What Will You Actually Use in 2027?

Consider your family's medications, chronic conditions, planned procedures, and potential hospital needs, not just monthly premiums.

If you're currently taking or considering GLP-1 medications for weight loss, FSBP may offer a significant advantage over FEP Blue, which has more restrictive coverage for these medications.

However, FSBP's patient cost-sharing for GLP-1 weight-loss medications will increase from 10% to 50% in 2027. Torii Health–Iris Dental Medical is working to keep monthly patient co-pay costs around $250–$450 per month. If this becomes unaffordable, please contact our team to discuss alternatives.

Patients obtaining Mounjaro through Japanese or other online clinics should also be aware that insurance coverage may require documentation of Type 2 diabetes. Without an appropriate diagnosis, claims may be denied.

For patients anticipating imaging, surgery, or hospitalization, FSBP generally does not require prior authorization or precertification for overseas care, with limited exceptions. This can make accessing medically necessary care easier, although claims remain subject to medical necessity and plan rules.

The Bottom Line

  • Regular healthcare needs: FSBP deserves serious consideration for its predictable coverage and straightforward claims process.
  • Infrequent healthcare needs: A lower-premium FEP Blue option may be more economical if you're comfortable with potentially higher out-of-pocket expenses.
  • GLP-1s, expensive medications, specialty-service needs, procedures, or hospitalizations: Compare coverage restrictions, ask your plan questions and review total expected costs carefully. Premiums are not the entire story here.

The lowest premium doesn't always mean the lowest (or best) healthcare costs for you and your family. Choose a plan based on how you and your family will actually use healthcare in Japan.
