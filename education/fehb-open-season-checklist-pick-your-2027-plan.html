Before December 14: How to Pick Your 2027 Health Plan Based on the Care You Actually Use

Nov 17, 2026 · Written by Dr. Kenji Taylor, MD · 4 min read

Less than four weeks of Open Season remain. Here's a practical way to compare plans using your family's real healthcare year, not just the monthly premium.

2027 FEHB Open Season

Enrollment closes December 14, 2026. New to comparing FSBP and FEP Blue? Start with Part 1 of our series: 2027 FEHB Open Season: Choosing the Right Health Plan in Japan.

Why the Premium Isn't the Whole Story

Back in the States, comparing health plans usually starts and ends with the monthly premium and the deductible. Living in Japan adds a layer most comparison charts don't show: most local providers expect you to pay up front and file your own claims, prescriptions can be harder to fill through US pharmacy benefits, and approvals for imaging or surgery can slow things down when your plan's rules weren't built with overseas care in mind.

FEHB Open Season runs through December 14, 2026. Whatever you choose now shapes how easy (or how frustrating) it is to get care for your family all through 2027. A few minutes of honest planning now can save you real money and paperwork later.

Start With Your Family's Real Healthcare Year

Before you open a single plan brochure, look back at 2026 and ahead to 2027. Jot down roughly how many office visits each family member had, any lab work or imaging, every regular prescription, and any counseling or therapy sessions.

Then think about what's coming: a planned surgery or procedure, a pregnancy, a chronic condition that needs regular monitoring, or a new medication you're considering. That short list is the real yardstick for comparing plans.

The question to ask

Not “Which plan has the lowest premium?” but “Which plan costs our family the least, in money and hassle, for the care we'll actually use in Japan?” The lowest premium doesn't always mean the lowest total cost.

How FSBP and FEP Blue Compare in Practice

We work with both the Foreign Service Benefit Plan (FSBP, administered by Aetna) and Blue Cross Blue Shield Federal (FEP Blue) every day. Hold your list up against these four areas:

  • Everyday outpatient care: FEP Blue Basic and Focus waive office-visit copays overseas, but what you pay for labs, imaging, and procedures varies by plan and service. FSBP generally covers 90% of eligible outpatient services after a modest deductible.
  • Prescriptions: FEP Blue's drug tiers, coverage restrictions, and pharmacy benefits can be hard to navigate from Japan. FSBP's medication benefits have been more predictable in our experience.
  • Approvals for bigger care: FSBP generally doesn't require prior authorization or precertification for overseas imaging, surgery, or hospitalization, with limited exceptions. Claims still have to meet medical-necessity and plan rules.
  • Claims and paperwork: We direct bill both plans for eligible medical and counseling services. In our experience, FSBP's overseas claims have gone through with fewer denials and fewer requests for extra documentation.

If your list is short, with a checkup or two and no regular medications, these differences may matter less. If it's long, they can add up quickly.

On a GLP-1 medication? Read this before you choose

FEP Blue has more restrictive coverage for GLP-1 weight-loss medications, so FSBP may still offer an advantage. But FSBP's patient share for these medications rises from 10% to 50% in 2027, which is a big jump to plan for. If you get Mounjaro through a Japanese or online clinic, be aware that coverage may require documentation of Type 2 diabetes; without it, claims may be denied. More on GLP-1s in Japan.

A Few Things Worth Keeping in Mind

  • This is our experience, not the official plan terms. All coverage depends on each plan's brochure, eligibility, medical necessity, and limitations. Always confirm the details that matter most to you with the plan itself.
  • A lower premium can still be the right call. If your family rarely needs care and you're comfortable with potentially higher out-of-pocket costs, a lower-premium FEP Blue option may be more economical.
  • Coverage changes from year to year. The 2027 GLP-1 change is a good reminder: last year's plan isn't automatically this year's best fit, even if nothing in your life has changed.
  • We're independent. Torii Health and Iris Dental Medical is a medical and dental practice, not an insurer or broker, and we're not sponsored by or affiliated with any insurance company. We encourage every patient to choose the plan that best fits their family's needs.

What You Can Actually Do

  • Make your list this week. Visits, labs, imaging, prescriptions, counseling, and anything planned for 2027, for every family member.
  • Check your medications first. Look up each regular prescription in each plan's coverage rules, especially GLP-1s and other high-cost drugs.
  • Ask the plans direct questions. How are overseas claims handled? Is prior authorization needed for the care you expect? What will your share be for your specific medications?
  • Don't wait until the last day. Open Season closes December 14, 2026. If you're a GLP-1 patient worried about 2027 costs, contact our team now so we can talk through alternatives.

The bottom line: The best 2027 plan is the one that fits how your family actually uses healthcare in Japan. Families with regular care needs should take a close look at FSBP's predictable coverage and claims; families with few needs may save with a lower premium. Either way, check your medications and expected care before December 14.
